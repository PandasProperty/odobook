What comes across

  • Vehicles, with their names and units (miles or kilometers, gallons or liters).
  • Every fill-up: date, odometer, amount, price, station and notes. Partial fill-ups and missed fill-ups keep their flags, so your MPG stays correct.
  • Every service: date, odometer, cost, the jobs done (oil change, tires, brakes…), the shop and your notes.

Odobook works out MPG, cost per mile and spending again from your fill-ups, with the full-tank method. The numbers may differ a little from what the old app showed, but every vehicle now follows the same rules. If you used Fuelly's trip meter instead of the odometer, Odobook rebuilds the odometer readings from the trip distances.

2

From the Fuelly website

  1. Sign in at fuelly.com and open your vehicle's Log Book.
  2. Choose Export Fuel-ups. You get a file called fuelups.csv.
  3. If you logged services, choose Export Services too. That file is services.csv.
  4. Repeat for each vehicle you have in Fuelly.
  5. Get the files onto your phone: save them to iCloud Drive or Google Drive, or email them to yourself and save the attachments to Files.
  6. In Odobook, go to Settings → Import from another app → Choose files and pick the files together.

Fuelly's website sometimes dates a fill-up one day late when it was logged in the evening. If Odobook spots that pattern, the preview offers to fix those dates.

3

From the Fuelly iPhone app

  1. In the Fuelly app, send yourself the CSV report by email. It's one file, usually named Fuelly Export.csv, with all your vehicles in it.
  2. Open the email on your phone and save the attachment to Files.
  3. In Odobook, go to Settings → Import from another app → Choose files and pick it.

This file doesn't say which units you used, so the preview asks for each vehicle.

4

From aCar

  1. In aCar, make a fresh full backup. aCar saves it as a file ending in .abp, in the aCar folder on your phone.
  2. If Odobook is on a different phone (for example, you're moving to an iPhone), copy the .abp file over. Google Drive works well for this.
  3. In Odobook, go to Settings → Import from another app → Choose files and pick the backup.

Fill-ups, services, units and vehicle details come across. aCar's other expenses and its standalone notes don't have a place in Odobook yet, and the preview tells you how many are left out before you save.

5

Check everything before it's saved

Nothing is written until you confirm. The preview lets you:

  • choose which vehicles to bring in, and rename them;
  • set or correct their units;
  • add a vehicle's history to one you already have in Odobook;
  • see how many fill-ups and services will be added, and anything that can't be read.

Importing the same file again adds nothing, because records already in Odobook are skipped. That makes it safe to export again later and bring in only what's new.

6

Questions

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The file is read on your phone and your log stays there. Odobook has no account and no sign-in. You can export to CSV or back up to a file whenever you like.

Does importing cost anything?

No. Importing, unlimited vehicles, the service log, reminders, export and backup are all free. An optional one-time Pro unlock adds charts, with no subscription.

My file wasn't recognized.

Odobook turns away a file it doesn't understand without changing anything. Send it to contact@tendmatehq.com and we'll look at it. Import problems are the ones we most want to hear about.

Can I keep using the old app while I try Odobook?

Yes. Exporting doesn't change anything in Fuelly or aCar. When you're ready to switch for good, export once more and import again: only the new records are added.

Odobook reads files you export from your own Fuelly or aCar account. It isn't affiliated with Fuelly, aCar or their owners. More help is on the support page.
